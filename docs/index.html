A machine-learning stock-picking engine for the S&P 500.

A supervised ranking system trained on liquidity, volatility and reversal signals to identify relative outperformers. XGBoost against a Ridge baseline, validated walk-forward with purge & embargo, and audited by a backtesting engine with a realistic transaction-cost model.

  1. 01

    Data

    505 S&P 500 tickers · 20 years of OHLCV, cleaned and repaired automatically.

  2. 02

    Features

    Amihud illiquidity, Garman-Klass & idiosyncratic volatility, reversal, momentum, VIX regime.

  3. 03

    Walk-forward

    9 expanding-window folds, 5-day purge & embargo. Optuna tuning on IC-IR, GPU training.

  4. 04

    Validation

    10,000-sample bootstrap, permutation test (p = 0.000), cost-sensitivity, sub-period stability.

Trading Infrastructure

Cost-aware backtesting engine

A vectorized simulation engine that evaluates portfolio constructions under realistic frictions: fixed commissions plus Amihud-calibrated slippage (~3 bps mean, 30 bps cap). It compared 7 incremental strategies over 2,269 trading days — and showed that managing turnover matters as much as the predictive signal itself.

Strategies  S0→S6 Days simulated  2,269

About me

Rigor first.
Then speed.

I'm a Computer Science student at Universitat Politècnica de València, specialized in artificial intelligence and quantitative finance. I started investing on demo accounts at sixteen; discovering that most institutional trading is systematic convinced me that engineering is the right way into markets.

My bachelor thesis — an ML stock-picking engine for the S&P 500 — reflects how I like to work: vectorized, reproducible code; validation methodologies that don't flatter the model; and results reported net of costs, with the statistics to back them. I've interned in quantitative services at EY and build trading systems with the UPV Investment Club.
